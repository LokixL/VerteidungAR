CHAPTER 13 · CONCLUSIONS & OUTLOOK

What can we conclude?

The thesis demonstrates a portable research platform. It does not yet validate an operational fire detector.

01 / DEMONSTRATED

Technical feasibility

A portable prototype was built and operated using passive recording, active stepped-frequency measurements and active deconvolution.

Repeated acquisition, external excitation verification and reproducible transfer responses support its role as a research platform.

02 / OBSERVED

Fire-related differences

In the evaluated gas-fire recording pair, a pronounced passive difference appeared around 6–7 kHz. Active deconvolution showed localized changes near 10.7–10.9 kHz.

These findings motivate further investigation, but the changes have not been shown to be unique to combustion.

03 / STILL OPEN

Validated detection

Reliable live classification, warning thresholds, false-alarm rates, missed detections and suitability for emergency operations remain to be demonstrated.

Future work includes independent recordings, realistic environments, more fire and non-fire conditions, and continued prototype improvement.

Final statement

RIPLEY is a working, reproducible acoustic research platform — a basis for the development and validation of future preventive fire detection.
